## Story

**As a support lead, I want control over when the bot speaks, so that automation matches how we operate.**

## Acceptance criteria

1. Auto-send can be restricted to configured business hours in the tenant's timezone.
2. A configurable delay is applied before auto-send, during which a human can intercept and cancel.
3. Specific intents can be excluded from auto-send entirely.
4. Volume caps limit auto-sends per hour per tenant.
5. Outside business hours, drafts queue and send at open rather than being discarded.

---

## 🔴 AC5 turns a decision made at 6pm into an action taken at 9am, and nothing re-checks it

`scheduled_for` is the mechanism for all three window lengths — the undo window (seconds), AC2's delay (minutes), and AC5's business-hours queue (**up to fourteen hours**). Delta 3 already noted the first two are the same mechanism, and this story adds an order of magnitude.

**A pending row is a decision frozen in time.** Everything that made auto-sending correct was evaluated when the row was written, and the drain (Story 6.1) claims it on `state = 'pending' AND scheduled_for <= now()` — **two conditions, neither of which is about whether the reply is still the right thing to send.**

Overnight, any of these ordinarily happens:

| Between 6pm and 9am | The queued auto-send now |
|---|---|
| An agent replied manually (Story 3.5) | Sends a **second** answer to a question already answered |
| The customer wrote again, changing the question | Answers the old one, confidently |
| The draft was regenerated (Story 5.5) | Sends the **superseded** version |
| The conversation was resolved | Reopens it with a bot reply |
| An admin disabled auto-send | Story 6.3 AC5 cancels `pending` rows — ✅ this one is covered |
| The contact hard-bounced (Story 6.5) | Sends to a known-invalid address |

Only the fifth has an owner. **The rest send, at 9am, and look exactly like a correct auto-send** — timeline event, `auto_sent` state, marked in analytics. The tenant's first impression of automation is a bot talking over its own team.

**Ruling: the claim re-validates preconditions; `scheduled_for` schedules, it does not authorise.**

Story 6.1's `outbound_claim_due` claims on time and state. **After the claim and inside `withTenant()`**, before handing to the connector, the sender re-checks:

1. The draft is still `proposed` or `auto_sent`-eligible — **not `superseded`, not `rejected`.** Story 5.5's partial unique index makes this one condition.
2. No outbound message has been sent on this conversation since the row was enqueued.
3. No inbound message has arrived since the row was enqueued.
4. The conversation is not `resolved`.
5. Auto-send is still enabled, still within business hours, and the intent is still not excluded.
6. The contact address is not suppressed (Story 6.5).

Any failure → `cancelled`, with a `conversation_events` row saying which precondition changed. **Not `dead`** — nothing failed, the world moved.

**The cost is one query per send and the alternative is a class of incident with no upper bound**, because the window is a tenant setting and a tenant can configure a weekend.

> **The general form, and the fourth instance this week.** A queue makes a decision durable, and durability is not the same as continued correctness. The check is: **what was true when this row was written that might not be true when it runs — and does anything look?**

## AC2's delay is the undo window under another name, and that is worth making literal

Front-End Spec §5.3 promises *"a 5-minute delay you can cancel within"*; delta 3 built cancel-within-window for manual send-undo. **Two features, one mechanism, two implementations is how they drift** — the manual one gets the affected-row-count honesty and the auto one silently does not, or vice versa.

One code path: `enqueue(outbound, delaySeconds)` and `cancel(outboundId)` returning affected rows, with the caller deciding the window. Manual undo passes seconds, auto-send passes AC2's minutes, business hours passes the interval to open. **The honesty in cancel — 1 means cancelled, 0 means already claimed, the toast says "already sent" — comes free at all three lengths.**

## AC4's "per hour per tenant" is a rate limit on a cron, which is where they usually go wrong

The drain runs every minute (§12) with `LIMIT 50`. A per-hour cap enforced by counting sends in the last hour **at claim time** is a read the claim cannot afford, and one enforced *after* the claim means the row is claimed, unsendable, and has to go somewhere.

Enforce it at **enqueue**, not at send: when the auto-send decision is made, count this tenant's auto-sends in the window and, over cap, push `scheduled_for` past it rather than cancelling. The cap becomes a throttle instead of a discard, which is what AC5's "queue rather than discard" already establishes as the epic's preferred failure direction.

`usage_records` (Epic 8) is the count's home, but it does not exist yet — this story counts `outbound_messages` with `state = 'auto_sent'` in the window, which is exact and cheap on `idx_outbox_pending`'s table.

## AC3's intent exclusion depends on Story 5.1's derivation rule

Intents are excluded from auto-send. **Which intent?** Story 5.1 ruled that `conversations.intent` is the **first** classified intent — what the customer originally wanted — while classification now lives per message.

For exclusion the relevant intent is the one on the **message being replied to**, not the conversation's summary. A thread that opens as `billing` and turns into `refund_request` must be excluded on `refund_request`, and the conversation-level value would say `billing`.

**This is the derived-summary rule paying off in the direction it was written for**, and it is easy to get wrong by reading the conversation because it is the column that is right there.

## AC1's timezone is a tenant attribute that does not exist

Business hours "in the tenant's timezone". `tenants` has `name`, `slug`, `clerk_org_id`, `plan`, `status`, `settings`, `region`, `created_at` — **no timezone**, and `region` is `'us-east'` with a single-value CHECK (§6.8d), which is an infrastructure location and not a business one. A London tenant on `us-east` keeps London hours.

Add `timezone text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'UTC'` to `tenants`, IANA names. **Defaulting to UTC is deliberate and slightly wrong for everyone**, which is the point — a tenant that has not set it gets hours that are obviously off rather than silently plausible, and §6.2's warning about defaults that lie applies: a default of `'America/New_York'` would look correct to the majority and be wrong invisibly for the rest.

DST is why this is an IANA name and not an offset. A stored `-05:00` is correct for half the year.

## Tasks

### Task 1 — Preconditions at claim (the ruling)

- [ ] Six checks after the claim, inside `withTenant()`, before the connector.
- [ ] Failure → `cancelled` plus a timeline event naming what changed.

### Task 2 — One scheduling mechanism (AC2, AC5)

- [ ] `enqueue(outbound, delaySeconds)` / `cancel(outboundId) → affectedRows`, shared by undo, delay, and business hours.

### Task 3 — Business hours (AC1, AC5)

- [ ] `tenants.timezone`, IANA, default `'UTC'`.
- [ ] Outside hours → `scheduled_for = next open`. Queue, never discard.

### Task 4 — Intent exclusion (AC3)

- [ ] Excluded on the **replied-to message's** intent, not the conversation summary.

### Task 5 — Volume cap (AC4)

- [ ] Enforced at enqueue; over cap pushes `scheduled_for`, does not cancel.
